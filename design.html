The Look

The visual language of the calendar, in one place. Everything below is defined by name in styles/tokens.css; later parts of the app use those names rather than choosing colours or sizes of their own. Switch your computer between light and dark mode — this page follows it.

The material

Everything raised is made of the same glass: a translucent fill, a blur that frosts whatever is behind it, a bright hairline edge, and a single lit line along the top where the light catches. It is one class, .glass, so the calendar card, the buttons and the event panel can never drift apart.

.glass
panels, buttons, the calendar itself
.glass--strong
denser, for the event panel

The colour wash behind the page is what the glass has to refract — it is fixed in place, so the material stays still while the page scrolls. On browsers with no blur support the glass falls back to a solid surface rather than a muddy translucent one.

Colour

Three pools of colour wash the backdrop. On top of them, solid ink and a single accent used sparingly — today's date, the save button, an event's colour bar.

Type

The system's own typeface — SF on a Mac or iPhone, Segoe on Windows. Nothing to download, so it looks right offline and on the very first paint. Large sizes are set tighter, the way the face is designed to be. Shown here at the exact sizes the app uses.

Space and shape

Every gap is a multiple of 4px, so the layout stays in rhythm. Shown to scale.

--space-1 through --space-8 · 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64px

Corners are generously round — glass has no sharp edges. Small for buttons and inputs, large for cards, extra large for the event panel.

Buttons

Quiet buttons are glass and lift towards the light when you point at them. The one action that matters is solid accent. One primary button is visible at a time.

Text boxes

Inputs are recessed rather than raised — the one place the design goes into the glass instead of sitting on it, so it reads as somewhere to type rather than something to press. An invalid box is marked by its outline and by the message, never by colour alone.

Contrast

Glass makes contrast a real question, because what is behind the text changes. It is answered by keeping the colour wash pale and the glass dense enough that the effective background stays within a known range — the figures below are measured against the worst case, the most saturated point of the wash seen through the glass, in both themes. Normal text needs 4.5, and every pair clears it.
